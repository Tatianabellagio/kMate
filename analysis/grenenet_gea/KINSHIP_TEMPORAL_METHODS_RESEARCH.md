## Question
Add a kinship/relatedness (population-structure) control to a PER-HAPLOBLOCK analysis of TEMPORAL allele-frequency change (replicated E&R / pool-seq, known founder panel), keeping temporal+replicate variance and ALLOWING block-specific residuals — i.e. NOT collapsing to a founder-level GWAS. Setup: GrENE-net, ~200 inbred founders, near-uniform founding, ~11 plots/site, gens 0-3; ~0 within-experiment recombination so whole ecotype genomes hitchhike and per-block selection is smeared by genome-wide founder winnowing.

## Recommended formulation (Omega-conditioned per-block temporal contrast)
1. **Omega** = among-population coancestry covariance from genome-wide block freqs (the structure to remove = whole-genome ecotype winnowing).
2. **Populations = site×generation (or plots×gen)** + seedmix(gen0) → puts time in the design.
3. Per block, standardize the population allele-freq vector and form the **C2 contrast** (founding vs evolved, or warm vs cold) conditioned on Omega → calibrated **chi2_1** per-block p/q. Mechanism = whiten freqs through Cholesky of Omega (GLS transform vs shared demography); MVN drift null `freq ~ N(p·1, p(1-p)·Omega)`.
4. **Drift scale** from among-replicate variance (ACER-style) feeds the null.
5. **Genome-wide check** with `cvtkpy`: fraction of Δp variance from linked selection (Buffalo-Coop got 17–37% in E&R) + across-replicate convergence correlation (shared vs divergent selection).

This is the FORMAL, calibrated version of the ad-hoc `local_h − global_h` residual idea: Omega-whitening = subtracting the genome-wide founder background, but with a proper per-block null, and a block beyond its coancestry expectation still scores (allows block-specific signal).

### BayPass (Omega / C2 / XtX)

- **[central]** BayPass models the neutral relatedness among populations via a scaled population covariance matrix Omega that explicitly incorporates the correlation structure of allele frequencies, i.e. a pop-gen analog of a GWAS kinship/coancestry matrix conditioning per-locus frequencies on shared demographic history.
- **[central]** BayPass yields a per-locus differentiation statistic XtX, defined as a SNP-specific FST that is explicitly corrected for the scaled covariance of population allele frequencies (Omega), giving a per-locus deviation test from a multivariate-normal null that includes the coancestry covariance.
- **[central]** The per-SNP standardized allele-frequency vector is given a multivariate Gaussian prior with covariance proportional to the inverse precision matrix Lambda^-1 (= Omega) scaled by pi(1-pi), so the null is an explicit MVN drift model that includes the founder/population covariance — directly analogous to GLS on frequencies with a coancestry covariance.
- **[central]** BayPass uses a population coancestry covariance matrix Omega (with precision matrix Lambda = its inverse) to model the neutral correlation structure of allele frequencies across populations, conditioning per-locus frequencies on shared demographic history so that drift can be separated from selection — the allele-frequency analog of a GWAS kinship matrix.
- **[central]** The XtX statistic is a per-locus, covariance-adjusted FST-like measure (an FST explicitly corrected for the scaled covariance Omega of population allele frequencies) that provides a calibrated per-locus test of departure from the neutral covariance-aware drift null, with thresholds set via pseudo-observed datasets simulated under that null.
- **[central]** The BayPass framework is built for spatially/demographically structured population allele-count data (including Pool-Seq read counts), NOT for temporal or replicate-time-series allele-frequency-change data; the data model is allele counts in sampled populations, so applying it to a temporal E&R design requires recasting timepoints/replicates as 'populations' rather than using a native temporal model.
- **[central]** BayPass yields locus-specific tests of departure from expectation under a neutral demographic (covariance-aware) model, i.e. a per-locus deviation test against a multivariate drift null that includes the among-population covariance structure.
- **[central]** BayPass's core model estimates Omega, the scaled covariance matrix of population allele frequencies arising from the populations' shared (demographic) history, and uses it as the structure-control analog of a GWAS kinship matrix; this is the same coancestry-covariance object the research question's method 1 targets.
- **[central]** The XtX differentiation statistic (Gunther and Coop 2013) is the covariate-free per-locus genome-scan statistic computed by default, and its advantage is explicitly that it conditions per-locus differentiation on the estimated Omega covariance structure — i.e., it standardizes loci against genome-wide shared structure, the operation needed to remove whole-genome founder background.
- **[central]** The C2 contrast statistic tests, per locus, a contrast of STANDARDIZED allele frequencies between two predefined groups of populations (group membership coded +1/-1/0), calibrated against a chi-square distribution with 1 df (Olazcuaga et al. 2020) — providing a per-locus group-contrast test that already conditions on Omega, directly mappable to contrasting evolved vs founder pools.
- **[central]** The C2 contrast statistic detects allele-frequency differentiation between two groups of populations defined by a binary trait/covariate (contrast vector c with cj=+1 for one group, -1 for the other) while explicitly correcting for population structure via the scaled covariance matrix Omega of population allele frequencies. This is directly the founder-coancestry-conditioned binary-contrast test we need (e.g. founding gen 0 vs evolved, or by climate group).
- **[central]** The structure correction works by standardizing per-locus allele frequencies through the Cholesky decomposition of Omega (Gamma_Omega from Omega^-1), i.e. a GLS/whitening transform of allele frequencies against the shared-demography covariance, so the test conditions each locus on genome-wide relatedness — the exact mechanism for removing whole-genome founder winnowing in our setting.
- **[central]** The underlying model is a multivariate Gaussian (GLS-style) drift null: instrumental allele frequencies across J populations are distributed N_J(pi*1_J, pi(1-pi)*Omega), so per-locus deviation is tested against a multivariate-normal null that embeds the among-population coancestry covariance.
- **[central]** GRoSS models neutral genome-wide drift among populations as a multivariate normal distribution whose covariance matrix F encodes the shared demographic structure (admixture graph), i.e. allele frequencies are conditioned on a coancestry-like covariance — the population-genomic analog of a GWAS kinship matrix.
- **[central]** By choosing the contrast vector b to correspond to a particular branch k of the admixture graph, the statistic S_B(k) localizes per-locus deviations from neutrality to a specific branch/contrast — a generalization beyond BayPass's single XtX score, which cannot localize when in population history a sweep occurred.
- **[central]** BayPass estimates a scaled population covariance matrix Omega across population allele frequencies that captures the neutral covariance/correlation structure (shared demographic history) among populations, and accounting for it is the core mechanism for robust genome-wide scans — directly analogous to a GWAS kinship/relatedness control for allele-frequency data.
- **[central]** Per-locus standardized allele frequencies are modeled as multivariate Gaussian with covariance determined by Omega (centered on an ancestral frequency), i.e. a multivariate-normal drift null conditioned on the shared covariance structure — the GLS/Mahalanobis-style conditioning the research question seeks.
- **[central]** The XtX statistic is a per-locus (SNP-specific) FST-like differentiation measure explicitly corrected for the scaled covariance of population allele frequencies, giving a per-locus test of deviation beyond the genome-wide structure — the per-block test the question needs, with calibration of XtX to call overly-differentiated loci.
- **[central]** BayPass models population allele frequencies with a multivariate Gaussian prior whose covariance is the scaled covariance matrix Omega (precision Lambda), which captures the neutral correlation structure across populations arising from shared evolutionary history/common demography — the pop-gen analog of a kinship/coancestry matrix used to condition per-locus frequencies on structure.
- **[central]** The XtX differentiation statistic is a SNP-specific FST explicitly corrected for the scaled covariance Omega of population allele frequencies, i.e. a per-locus standardized (Mahalanobis-like) contrast that removes the genome-wide shared-history structure so locus-specific differentiation can be detected beyond the neutral background.
- **[central]** BayPass computes XtX per locus by quadratic-form standardization using the precision matrix Lambda at each MCMC iteration, dividing by the ancestral-frequency variance term pi(1-pi) — a concrete per-locus test conditioning on the among-population covariance.
- **[supporting]** The STD covariate model adds a per-locus regression term beta_i*Z (population-specific covariate) on top of the Omega-conditioned MVN background, providing a per-locus environment/covariate-association test that conditions out shared structure; the AUX model adds a binary inclusion indicator and an Ising spatial prior allowing LD among neighboring loci.
- **[supporting]** BayPass is designed for spatial population samples sharing a common history, not temporal/replicate designs — the worked Pool-Seq example uses 12 spatial Littorina saxatilis populations across three geographic regions, so adapting it to temporal generation/replicate data is non-native and would require restructuring populations as time points.
- **[supporting]** BayPass provides an auxiliary-variable (AUX) model that introduces a binary indicator delta_i per SNP to test SNP-by-covariate association while formally handling multiple testing by integrating over the uncertainty in the association probability P.
- **[supporting]** BayPass is explicitly intended to apply beyond standard population-genetics studies to experimental-evolution data, where the allele-frequency covariance structure is simpler — supporting (but not natively implementing temporal slopes) the adaptation to an evolve-and-resequence founder-panel setting.
- **[supporting]** The companion calibrated XtX* statistic rescales the standardized allele frequencies (centered/scaled by the across-SNP mean and SD of posterior means) before forming XtX = alpha'alpha, providing a properly calibrated overall per-locus differentiation measure distinct from the contrast-specific C2.
- **[supporting]** GRoSS outperforms BayPass (XtX) at detecting selection in their evaluation, and unlike BayPass yields branch/contrast-resolved per-locus signals rather than a single genome-wide-structure-corrected score.
- **[supporting]** The covariance object used is a time-by-time (τ×τ) standardized variance-covariance matrix Q computed by averaging over many neutral loci, i.e. the structure is over TIME INTERVALS, not over founders/loci, and it is estimated empirically from the data rather than from a founder-coancestry kinship matrix.

### Buffalo & Coop temporal covariance

- **[central]** The method estimates the genome-wide contribution of selection (a lower bound G(t) on the proportion of total allele-frequency-change variance caused by linked selection) by summing temporal covariances of allele-frequency change across loci, not by testing individual loci.
- **[central]** Selection signal is undetectable at single loci because drift and sampling noise swamp the temporal covariance; it only emerges after aggregating across the genome, so the method does NOT natively yield a per-locus/per-block test.
- **[central]** cvtkpy implements the methods of Buffalo & Coop (2019, 2020) to compute temporal covariances of allele-frequency change and convergence correlations from temporal genomic data.
- **[central]** The package's underlying method is the Buffalo & Coop (2020 PNAS) approach to estimating the genome-wide contribution of selection to temporal allele-frequency change, which is the recommended citation for the newer methods.
- **[central]** The package targets genome-wide summary statistics (temporal covariances, convergence correlations) rather than per-locus tests; the README documents no per-locus testing API.
- **[central]** Genetic drift acting alone produces zero expected temporal covariance between allele-frequency changes at neutral loci, whereas linked selection generates positive covariance — providing a drift-null baseline against which selection signal is detected.
- **[central]** The method is fundamentally a genome-wide (averaging-over-loci) estimator rather than a per-locus test: at any single locus drift and sampling noise can swamp or spuriously create temporal covariance, and only averaging across the genome makes the selection signal exceed sampling noise (because spurious covariances lack a shared directional signal).
- **[central]** The method detects/quantifies linked selection from the genome-wide covariance between allele-frequency changes across timepoints, exploiting that heritable fitness variation makes early AF changes predictive of later changes (whereas drift produces zero temporal covariance) — this is a genome-wide signal, not a per-locus test.
- **[central]** Because genetic drift produces no temporal covariance, the temporal covariance can be used to estimate the FRACTION of total allele-frequency-change variance attributable to linked selection — a variance-decomposition (drift vs linked selection) rather than a per-locus selection-coefficient test.
- **[central]** The temporal-covariance signal of linked selection is readily distinguishable from genetic drift on short timescales (few generations) in replicated temporal data, supporting its use as a drift-null contrast in E&R-style designs like the GrENE-net temporal pool-seq setup.
- **[central]** The method uses genome-wide COVARIANCE between allele-frequency changes across timepoints (not a per-locus statistic) to detect the contribution of linked selection; because drift produces zero temporal covariance, the covariance isolates the selection component. This makes the core estimator a genome-wide aggregate quantity, not a native per-locus/per-block test.
- **[central]** The temporal covariance arises specifically from linked selection: alleles change in frequency indirectly because of remaining (linkage) associations with selected alleles, which is exactly the whole-genome-hitchhiking / structure background the GrENE-net per-block analysis needs to remove.
- **[central]** The Buffalo & Coop temporal-covariance method quantifies linked selection at the GENOME-WIDE level, not as a per-locus or per-block test; it partitions the genome into nonoverlapping windows but the estimand is the genome-wide effect of linked selection.
- **[central]** Heritable variation in fitness produces temporal autocovariance in neutral allele-frequency change across generations, which is the signal that distinguishes linked selection from pure drift in temporal data.
- **[supporting]** The covariance object is built directly from pooled allele frequencies (no founder panel or haplotype reconstruction required), specifically a genome-wide temporal covariance matrix Q corrected for sampling-noise bias and normalized for heterozygosity.
- **[supporting]** Temporal covariance Cov(Δps,Δpt) between allele-frequency change over an early interval and a later interval is created by heritable fitness variation, making early changes predictive of later changes — directly relevant to modeling temporal trajectories across generations 0-3.
- **[supporting]** Replicate populations under similar selection produce between-replicate (convergence) covariances by the same mechanism as temporal covariance, providing a replicate-based handle on shared (structure-like) versus divergent selection.
- **[supporting]** The package handles replicated and temporal data, computing covariances across time intervals and convergence correlations across replicates.
- **[supporting]** They natively use replicated + multi-timepoint data, estimating a full temporal covariance matrix Q per replicate from multiple time points across replicate populations.
- **[supporting]** The framework yields a genome-wide quantitative estimate of the fraction of allele-frequency change attributable to selection (17–37%), not a locus-resolved selection map.
- **[supporting]** Negative temporal covariances are interpretable: they correspond to reversals in the direction of selection over the time course for a proportion of loci, meaning the estimator can capture sign/direction structure of selection over time intervals (relevant to multi-generation temporal modeling), though still as an aggregate signal.
- **[supporting]** The estimator partitions the variance in allele-frequency change into a drift component versus a linked-selection component, quantifying the fraction attributable to selection; in the three reanalyzed E&R experiments at least 17%-37% of allele-frequency change is driven by selection.
- **[supporting]** Negative temporal covariances are detectable and correspond to reversals in the direction of selection over the time course, showing the covariance signal can resolve direction-of-selection structure beyond a single genome-wide positive value (relevant to whether block-specific signals can deviate from the background).
- **[tangential]** Documentation/usage examples are external (in the cvtk repository notebooks from the 2020 PNAS paper); the README itself contains no concrete API beyond an import smoke-test.

### ACER / CMH drift nulls

- **[central]** The adapted CMH test (acer package) modifies the standard Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test so that its variance term incorporates both genetic drift variance and pool-sequencing sampling variance, rather than assuming pure binomial sampling.
- **[central]** The adapted CMH test requires the effective population size (Ne) per replicate population and the sampled generations as inputs, encoding a drift model parameterized by Ne and elapsed generations.
- **[central]** The adapted CMH test natively handles replicated evolve-and-resequence time-series data, combining multiple time points (generations) and replicate populations into one per-SNP test.
- **[central]** The adapted CMH test produces a per-locus (per-SNP) selection test statistic with associated p-value, i.e. it is a per-locus temporal selection test rather than only a genome-wide summary.
- **[central]** ACER's adapted CMH and chi-squared tests correct the test variance for genetic drift and pool-sequencing noise, addressing the overdispersion that makes the classical tests invalid for E&R temporal allele-frequency data.
- **[central]** ACER produces a per-locus (per-SNP) test, outputting one p-value (and test statistic) per locus, making it applicable as a per-block temporal test if blocks are treated as units.
- **[supporting]** The adapted CMH test takes per-timepoint per-replicate pool sequencing sample sizes (poolSize) to model the pool-seq sampling variance, and this can be omitted (NULL) when data are not pool-sequenced.
- **[supporting]** ACER models drift via a user-supplied effective population size Ne and a generations vector; if generations are constant (length-1 or repeated), no drift component is applied, so drift correction is a tunable component.
- **[supporting]** The adapted CMH test natively combines multiple replicate populations through a repl parameter, supporting the replicate structure of E&R experiments.
- **[supporting]** The method identifies candidate SNPs using the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) and chi-square tests with a drift + pool-sequencing variance correction as implemented in the ACER R package, rather than a simulation-based or dedicated block-level temporal null.
- **[tangential]** ACER does not estimate Ne internally; the effective population size must be supplied by the user as a parameter to both tests.

### LFMM2 / latent-factor

- **[central]** LFMM 2.0 estimates confounders (latent factors representing unobserved population structure) jointly with the primary effect using a least-squares latent factor model, rather than as a separate heuristic preprocessing step like principal components or surrogate variable analysis.
- **[central]** LFMM 2.0 is applicable to gene-environment association studies and was used to study adaptation of humans to their environment, demonstrating the latent-factor framework for environmental (GEA-type) association with population-structure correction.
- **[central]** LFMM2 fits a per-locus multivariate linear regression of the genomic response on the environmental covariate plus estimated latent factors, yielding a per-locus association test that conditions on confounding structure.
- **[central]** The latent matrix W (rank K, via latent factors U) represents unobserved confounders such as population structure/ancestry, acting as the population-structure/relatedness control analogous to a kinship correction in GEA.
- **[supporting]** Existing confounder-adjustment methods (e.g. PCA-based and SVA approaches) ignore correlation between confounders and primary variables, which reduces statistical power and precision; LFMM 2.0 is designed to address this.
- **[supporting]** In simulations, the LFMM 2.0 least-squares algorithms outperformed commonly used confounder-correction methods based on principal components and surrogate variable analysis.
- **[supporting]** LFMM2 uses least-squares minimization (not MCMC), making it 10,000-100,000 times faster than LFMM 1.5 and scalable to genome-wide data.
- **[tangential]** The LFMM 2.0 algorithms are computationally fast/efficient and provably optimal, and are implemented in the openly available R package 'lfmm'.
- **[tangential]** LFMM2 is extended to handle data types beyond individual genotypes (e.g., methylation, gene expression), indicating the response can be a general quantitative genomic matrix rather than strictly genotype calls.

### Haplotype-block reconstruction

- **[central]** haplovalidate reconstructs haplotype blocks by hierarchically clustering SNPs whose allele-frequency trajectories are highly correlated across replicates and time points within overlapping genomic windows, exploiting that linked/hitchhiking SNPs co-vary over time.
- **[central]** Collapsing correlated hitchhiker SNPs into haplotype blocks resolves the inflation of per-SNP E&R analyses, where the number of outlier SNPs exceeds what population-genetic theory permits, by reducing thousands of candidate SNPs to a few selected regions.

### GWAS LMM / kinship

- **[central]** The standard GWAS LMM models the phenotype as y = mu + X*beta + Z*u + e with a polygenic random effect u ~ N(0, sigma_g^2 * K), where K is a genome-wide genetic similarity (kinship) matrix between all pairs of individuals, and this single mixed model simultaneously accounts for population structure, known kinship, and cryptic relatedness.
- **[central]** Including the genome-wide kinship random effect is mathematically equivalent to including all principal components of relatedness (weighted by their singular values) in the model while estimating only a small number of free variance parameters, which is how the LMM separates a per-SNP fixed effect from the genome-wide polygenic background structure.
- **[supporting]** The kinship matrix K is a simple function of observed genome-wide genotypes (e.g. the Balding-Nichols metric used in EMMAX, computed from ~45,000 markers pruned from 650,000), with entry K_ij representing the genetic similarity between individuals i and j.
- **[supporting]** Population structure and kinship are forms of genetic relatedness acting at different scales, and the LMM with a genome-wide similarity matrix is the established correction for these confounders in association testing.
- **[tangential]** The paper proposes a Low Rank LMM (LRLMM) extension that learns the effective dimensionality of the population-structure/kinship correction rather than fixing it, and introduces an effective-degrees-of-freedom metric (edf = Cov(yhat,y)/sigma_e^2) that quantifies how much of the variance the relatedness correction absorbs.

### Other / cross-cutting

- **[central]** The approach does not provide a formal per-block selection significance test; blocks are formed by clustering pre-selected candidate SNPs and validated post-hoc by checking that within-block (focal) trajectory correlations exceed between-chromosome (background) correlations.
